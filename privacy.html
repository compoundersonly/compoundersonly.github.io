Privacy Policy

Last updated: 18 October 2026

Shorts Poster is a personal tool. Its owner runs it on their own computer to upload their own short videos to their own YouTube channel, Instagram account and TikTok account. It isn't offered to anyone else, and nobody else signs in to it.

What it accesses

  • YouTube (YouTube API Services): permission to upload videos and set their thumbnails, and read access to the channel's name and the status of videos it uploaded.
  • Instagram (Instagram Graph API): the Instagram professional account and the Facebook Page linked to it, and permission to publish Reels to that account.
  • TikTok (Login Kit and Content Posting API): basic profile information (display name) and permission to upload videos to the account.

How it's used

Only to publish the owner's videos and to show the owner whether each post succeeded and where it is. Nothing is used for advertising, profiling or any other purpose.

Where it's stored

Sign-in tokens and upload records are stored only on the owner's computer. The tool has no server, and nothing it receives from Google, Meta or TikTok is sent to the developer or anyone else.

Sharing

Data received from these platforms is never sold, shared or transferred. To write titles and captions, the tool sends the video's own transcript and a few still frames from the owner's video file to Anthropic's Claude AI model. It doesn't send any data it received from Google, Meta or TikTok.

Google and YouTube

Shorts Poster uses YouTube API Services. By using it you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google's handling of data is described in the Google Privacy Policy. Shorts Poster's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access and data

  • Google / YouTube: remove Shorts Poster on the Google security settings page.
  • Instagram / Facebook: remove it under Business integrations in Facebook settings.
  • TikTok: remove it in the TikTok app's settings under app permissions.

Once access is removed, the stored sign-in tokens stop working, and the owner deletes them along with the upload records by deleting the tool's data folder.
